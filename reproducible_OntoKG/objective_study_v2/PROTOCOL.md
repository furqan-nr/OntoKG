# Objective audit-performance study — prepared, not run

This protocol has not been registered or executed. It supplies no new participant findings. The original 17-person fixed-order survey remains exploratory evidence of perceived trust/completeness only. Do not describe its ratings as objective audit performance.

## Question and endpoints

Does organizing the same analytical evidence into explicit metric roles improve verification of a claim compared with an ungrouped observation display?

Primary endpoint: each participant's proportion of correctly classified claims (valid, invalid, or incomplete evidence), averaged separately over six A and six B tasks. Primary contrast: participant-level accuracy B minus A. Secondary endpoints: correct identification of a supplied workbook/cell source, and participant-level median observed task time. Report accuracy and time separately; fast incorrect judgments are not successful verification.

The endpoint concerns consistency with supplied records, not authentication of external publications, analyst investment skill, or predictive quality.

## Stimuli and controls

The generator freezes twelve distinct company/window cases from an explicitly hashed graph: four supported claims, four refuted claims, and four cases with a required benchmark withheld. Missing-evidence cases are chosen so the visible company-versus-sector comparison does not already refute the claim. Missing values are not replaced by zero. These withheld-input interventions are designed stimuli, not newly discovered market events.

Display A lists ungrouped observations, values and window identifiers. Display B groups those same observations by analytical role. The source appendix is identical for a given case; neither display supplies a diagnostic validator verdict or SHACL badge. Both have the same claim and decision rule. Thus this tests evidence organization, rather than giving one condition privileged source access. Each participant sees a case once, in one condition, avoiding repeated exposure to the same answer.

Four prepared sequences counterbalance display assignment and forward/reverse case order. Each participant receives six tasks per condition and two tasks of each truth/evidence class per condition. Allocate successive participants in randomized blocks of four to S1–S4 using a recorded seed; do not select the sequence based on participant ability. Retain allocation and deviations privately.

## Execution

1. Confirm institutional approval/exemption and participant consent cover this changed protocol. The earlier survey's exemption must not automatically be claimed to cover new tasks or timing data.
2. Freeze the graph, task manifest, instructions, endpoint definitions and analysis plan before collecting responses. Register the plan if the authors intend to call it preregistered; a local Markdown document is not registration.
3. Pilot instructions with people excluded from the confirmatory sample. Adjust ambiguous wording before freezing; retain pilot changes separately.
4. Determine recruitment size using an explicit precision/power plan based on objective task accuracy, not the previous trust-rating effect. A small feasibility sample may be reported as a pilot, not a powered test.
5. Recruit consenting adults with the stated finance/data-auditing background. Record anonymous participant ID, role and experience only as justified by consent. Do not send contact information to the assistant or public repository.
6. Present one allocated packet. Record time from full task display to final submission consistently; note interruptions. Capture judgment and source-cell response before revealing answers.
7. Keep the facilitator answer key separate from participant packets. Supply neutral instructions; do not tell participants which display is expected to win.
8. Record attrition, interruptions, missing answers and allocation deviations. Do not silently drop incomplete participants or selectively report only favorable tasks.

## Analysis and reporting

The accompanying analyzer validates allocation, unique cases, consent, completeness and positive observed elapsed times. It refuses an empty response template. Its primary output is paired participant-level accuracy difference with a seeded participant bootstrap interval. It reports source identification and time per participant separately. Participant-level aggregation avoids counting all task answers as independent people.

The script's current complete-case analysis requires all twelve tasks; prospectively define missing-data and attrition reporting before a confirmatory run. Bootstrap intervals from very small samples can be unstable. Preserve all outcomes, error types and condition-by-task summaries; consider a preregistered mixed-effects analysis if the collected sample supports it. Do not add inferential claims merely because an interval excludes zero after exploratory changes.

Participant-level analysis output is written beside the supplied response file, not automatically into a public folder. Publish only consent-compatible aggregates. The supplied template contains a header and no participant data. Automated software smoke tests are not human trials and cannot establish improved audit performance.

## Consent text for institutional review

You are invited to evaluate how analytical evidence is displayed. Participation is voluntary, you may stop at any time, and you may decline without penalty. We will record your task judgments, source-record identifications and time taken, along with an anonymous study identifier and agreed background information. Some examples deliberately contain incomplete or inconsistent evidence. This evaluates the display and workflow, not your professional competence. Responses will be reported in aggregate under the approved research protocol. No investment decision is requested. Confirm that you are at least 18 years old and consent to participate before beginning.

This is draft consent language for author/institutional review, not a claim of ethical approval or completed consent.
