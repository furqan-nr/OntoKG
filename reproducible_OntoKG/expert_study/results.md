# User study — results (executed, n = 17; participant-level analysis)

Within-subject design; 8 real OntoKG-EQ statements; 7-point scales. Version A = result-only note,
Version B = same statement with its provenance-grounded evidence bundle. Google Form, anonymous.
**Analysis is at the participant level** (each participant contributes one mean per condition, n = 17)
to avoid pseudoreplication of the 136 nested item ratings.

**Participants (n = 17):** 6 finance professionals, 5 academics, 3 students, 3 other; median 5 years'
experience (range 1–16).

| Measure | A (mean) | B (mean) | Δ (95% CI, clustered bootstrap) | Test (participant-level) |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Trust | 2.93 | 5.79 | +2.87 [2.10, 3.51] | Wilcoxon p ≈ 5×10⁻⁴ (n=17) |
| Completeness of justification | 2.10 | 5.44 | +3.35 [2.60, 3.99] | Wilcoxon p ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ (n=16) |
| Preference | — | 16/17 prefer B on all 8 items; 17/17 on a majority | — | sign test p ≈ 1.5×10⁻⁵ |

Finance-professional subgroup (n=6): trust Δ = +3.42 (same direction, larger).

**Caveats:** single-session convenience panel (only 6 finance professionals); within-subject order shows A
before the more-detailed B, so part of the lift may be an information/order effect. A counterbalanced,
three-condition replication (result only; result + non-provenance detail; result + provenance) with a
mixed-effects ordinal model and time-to-verify measures is the appropriate next step. Reported as
promising evidence of practical utility, not a definitive causal result. Raw responses: `Form responses (17).xlsx`.

NOTE: an earlier version of this file reported item-level p-values (~10^-22); those were pseudoreplicated
(treating 136 nested ratings as independent) and are superseded by the participant-level analysis above.
